# S8: a procedural toon-shaded ship through S2

Run on fugaku, 2026-10-19, Godot 4.7.2-stable, Compatibility renderer only, rendered by the S2 harness under Xvfb with llvmpipe (R11, FT-AUD-016, §9.5).

## The ship

`spikes/s8/ship.gd` (`S8Ship`) builds the ship in code from engine primitives: a capsule fuselage with a nose cap and a hull ring, a bubble cockpit, two swept box wings, two capsule engine pods, a tail fin, and emissive engine glows. It uses no third-party asset, so `content/provenance.json` needs no entry. The parts take their colours from a palette by role (hull, accent, glass, glow); `variant` 0 is cream and red, `variant` 1 is mint and violet.

Shading has two passes:

- `spikes/s8/toon.gdshader` overrides `light()` with three hard bands ($N \cdot L > 0.5$ full, $> 0.05$ at 60 %, else 25 %) on top of a flat ambient, and adds a rim highlight in `EMISSION` where $1 - N \cdot V > 0.68$.
- `spikes/s8/outline.gdshader` is an inverted hull: an unshaded, front-culled copy of each part pushed out 0.05 units along its normals, in a dark violet. For the copy, each vertex normal is replaced by the mean of the distinct face normals at its position, so boxes and cylinder rims get a closed hull instead of gaps at their hard edges. Glows have no outline.

`spikes/s8/ship_view.tscn` puts the ship at the origin with a camera orbiting it by `yaw_degrees` and `pitch_degrees`, a key light fixed to the camera (so every angle is lit the same way), a flat dusk-blue background, and 4x MSAA on the viewport for the duration of the shot.

## Findings

- Custom `light()` shaders, front-face culling and MSAA all work in the Compatibility renderer under llvmpipe, with no warnings.
- The outline background must contrast with the outline colour: against the first, near-black background the outline vanished except where it crossed the ship.
- Outlines on the thin wing are thinner than on the rounded parts, since a box corner's mean normal is diagonal and pushes each face out by only $0.05/\sqrt{3}$; a constant screen-space width would need the offset in a custom vertex attribute. A faint seam shows on the hull ring in the side view.
- The rim highlight is subtle on the pale hull colours at this size; raising `rim_strength` or tinting it would make it read.
- Building the outline copies in GDScript costs about 0.3 s per ship at load; a real ship would cache the meshes or bake them.

Choosing a style is the owner's step (T016).

## v2: semi-realistic

After v1 the owner kept variant B's mint and violet but asked for a less cartoonish, slightly more photorealistic ship, still procedural and Compatibility-only. v1 (`ship.gd`, `ship_view.tscn`, `shots.json`) is unchanged above; v2 sits beside it.

### What changed

| | v1 | v2 |
|---|---|---|
| Hull | capsule, 2.6 units long | lofted superellipse sections, 5 units long, 1 wide, flattened top and sides |
| Cockpit | bubble as wide as the hull | small flush canopy, a fifth of the hull's width |
| Wings and fins | thick boxes | thin swept plates (custom meshes with flat normals), twin canted tail fins |
| Engines | capsule pods on the wings | slim nacelles beside the tail with intake rings, cooling fins, metal nozzles, a central main engine |
| Detail | none | 26 seeded greebles on the dorsal side, a graphite spine, side vents, nose thruster blocks, antenna, wingtip pods |
| Shading | toon `light()`, rim, inverted-hull outline | `StandardMaterial3D` (PBR), no outline, no rim term |
| Palette | mint, violet, yellow glass | sage hull, dark violet-graphite accents, metal greys, dark tinted glass, mint engine glow |
| Light | one key light, flat colour ambient | key (with shadows), cool fill and mint back light, all fixed to the camera; procedural sky for ambient light and reflections; AgX tonemapping |

`spikes/s8/ship_v2.gd` (`S8ShipV2`) builds the ship; `spikes/s8/ship_v2_view.tscn` frames it like v1's view (same yaw and pitch per shot, camera 8.2 units away). Nothing is third-party, so `content/provenance.json` still needs no entry.

### Materials

Six materials by role, all `StandardMaterial3D`:

| Role | Colour | Metallic | Roughness |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hull | sage (0.38, 0.47, 0.42) | 0.35 | 0.45 |
| Accent | violet-graphite (0.2, 0.18, 0.26) | 0.5 | 0.5 |
| Metal | grey (0.52, 0.53, 0.55) | 0.9 | 0.35 |
| Dark (intakes, vents) | (0.1, 0.1, 0.12) | 0.6 | 0.6 |
| Glass | (0.12, 0.15, 0.17) | 0 | 0.12 |
| Glow | mint, unshaded, emission 2.0 | – | – |

The four opaque non-glass roles share three 256x256 textures generated in GDScript from a fixed seed: an albedo tint (hull plating panels in staggered rows, each panel a few percent lighter or darker, 2-pixel seams), a roughness map (seams and grime rougher) and a normal map made from the panel height with `Image.bump_map_to_normal_map`, so the seams catch the light. They are applied triplanar, one tile per 1.6 units, so primitives and custom meshes share the same plating scale without UV work. Generating them takes about 0.1 s per ship (headless, 2 CPUs).

`wear` (0 or 1) is a variation: at 1 the panel shades vary more and low-frequency grime (blotches plus faint streaks from tileable value noise) darkens the albedo by up to 30 % and raises the roughness.

### Lighting

The key light (warm white, energy 2.2) casts directional shadows; the fill (cool blue, 0.45) and the back light (mint, 0.8) don't. The back light replaces v1's rim term with a physically lit edge. A `ProceduralSkyMaterial` (dark navy to slate, 64-pixel radiance) gives ambient light and metal reflections, while the background stays a flat slate blue (0.13, 0.15, 0.2) so the ship's silhouette reads; a visible sky gradient put a hard horizon band across the side view.

Tonemapping works in the Compatibility renderer. On the front view, against Filmic, Linear changes 15,748 pixels, ACES 16,746 and AgX 18,016 (measured on an earlier light setup). AgX gave the most muted, least saturated sage, closest to the brief, so v2 uses it. Turning the key light's shadows off changes 7,333 pixels (max difference 129), so shadows do render on llvmpipe.

### Compatibility renderer limits

- No screen-space ambient occlusion, SSIL, SSR, SDFGI, VoxelGI or volumetric fog in Compatibility. The missing ambient occlusion is the most visible gap: greebles and the spine sit on the hull without contact darkening. Baking a darker band into the albedo under them, or vertex-colour AO, would stand in for it.
- Glow (bloom) exists in Compatibility but v2 doesn't use it; the engine glows are flat unshaded discs.
- Directional shadows, normal maps, triplanar mapping, metallic/roughness, sky radiance reflections and all tonemappers (Linear, Filmic, ACES, AgX) worked with no warnings.
- Reflections come from the procedural sky only; there is nothing nearby to reflect, so the metal reads mostly through its highlights.
