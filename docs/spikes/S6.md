# S6: GDScript sim speed and far-from-origin precision

Run on fugaku (i7-11800H, 16 threads, `powersave` governor with turbo, no GPU used), 2026-10-19, Godot 4.7.2-stable official build (float32 `Vector3`, 64-bit GDScript `float`), gdUnit4 6.2.2. Risks R6 (FT-ARC-006, FT-ARC-009) and R16 (FT-SIM-035, D98). Both pass.

## R6: 100 ships and 500 swept projectiles at 60 Hz

### Method

The spike world exists in two layouts with the same rules, constants and arithmetic:

- **Packed** (`spikes/s6/s6_world_packed.gd`, `S6WorldPacked`): one packed array per field indexed by entity id (`PackedVector3Array` for positions, velocities, angular velocities, inputs and limits, `PackedFloat32Array` for mass, `PackedInt32Array` for site and ttl), plus a typed `Array[Quaternion]`, since Godot has no packed quaternion array.
- **Object** (`spikes/s6/s6_world_object.gd`, `S6WorldObject`): one `RefCounted` per ship and projectile, in typed arrays ordered by id.

Each tick runs four phases, all in id order with one seeded `RandomNumberGenerator` (FT-ARC-008, FT-ARC-009):

1. **Ships**: an assist-like controller per ship (velocity hold towards a random waypoint and face assist, both as body-frame thrust and torque clamped per axis to the ship's limits), then 6DOF integration: $x \mathrel{+}= v\,\Delta t + \tfrac12 a\,\Delta t^2$ (FT-SIM-038), Euler's equations with diagonal inertia for the body-frame angular velocity, and $q \leftarrow \mathrm{normalise}(q \cdot (\tfrac12\omega\,\Delta t, 1))$.
2. **Broadphase**: a uniform grid with 250 m cells keyed by site id and cell coordinates (a `Dictionary[int, Array]`), rebuilt every tick.
3. **Ship pairs**: each ship checks only the cells its 150 m proximity sphere overlaps (about 10 of the 27 neighbours) and records proximity warnings and contacts as events (FT-SIM-009, FT-SIM-010). There is no collision response.
4. **Projectiles**: each moves, then its segment is swept against the ship spheres in the cells its padded bounding box overlaps, using the motion relative to each ship over the tick (closest approach on the relative segment, ship radius 25 m plus projectile radius 0.5 m). The earliest hit is recorded as an event and the projectile is refired at once from a random ship at another with first-order lead at 1,500 m/s, so 500 stay live; misses expire after 3 s.

The 100 ships (50 t, up to 20 m/s² forward) start at random points and orientations in a 4 km cube and fly at up to 150 m/s; the scenario produces about 225 hits per second and a few contacts.

```
godot=$(tools/get-godot.sh)
taskset -c 0 $godot --headless --path . -s res://spikes/s6/bench.gd -- packed 3600 6
taskset -c 0 $godot --headless --path . -s res://spikes/s6/bench.gd -- object 3600 6
```

The script runs 600 warm-up ticks, then times 3,600 ticks (one minute of game time) with `Time.get_ticks_usec()`. Other spikes were running on other cores, so each layout ran 3 times on a quiet machine (load 0.8) and 3 times with the machine busy (load about 4), all on core 0.

### Results

Tick time in ms, seed 6:

| Layout | Machine | Mean | p50 | p99 | Max |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Packed | quiet | 1.13, 1.15, 1.17 | 1.11, 1.14, 1.15 | 1.42, 1.39, 1.83 | 2.64, 2.15, 3.36 |
| Object | quiet | 1.41, 1.40, 1.40 | 1.37, 1.38, 1.38 | 2.30, 1.78, 1.75 | 3.46, 3.09, 2.58 |
| Packed | busy | 1.32, 1.34, 1.38 | 1.31, 1.32, 1.37 | 1.69, 1.91, 1.72 | 3.58, 3.57, 2.74 |
| Object | busy | 1.60, 1.57, 1.74 | 1.59, 1.57, 1.72 | 2.07, 2.00, 2.27 | 4.33, 2.91, 3.43 |

Seeds 7 and 8 (quiet) gave packed means of 1.19 and 1.19 ms (p99 1.88, 1.67) and object means of 1.43 and 1.44 ms (p99 2.46, 2.20). Both layouts give the same hits, contacts and position checksum for every seed, so they compute the same thing.

Where the packed tick goes (quiet, mean per tick): ships 0.08 ms, grid rebuild 0.08 ms, ship pairs 0.41 ms, projectiles 0.64 ms. The first version checked all 27 neighbour cells per ship and allocated an empty array per missed lookup; that cost 1.03 ms in ship pairs and gave a mean of 1.85 ms (object 2.25 ms).

Scaling (packed, busy machine): 200 ships and 1,000 projectiles took 2.45 ms mean and 4.06 ms p99 (object 4.13 and 6.03 ms); 300 ships and 1,500 projectiles took 5.31 ms mean.

### Verdict

**Pass.** Both layouts are well under 4 ms in mean and p99; the packed layout is about 20 % faster and leaves about 2.6 ms of a 4 ms budget. A few single ticks reached 3.5–4.3 ms while other spikes loaded the machine; they don't show in p99. No fallback is needed. The ships phase is cheap, so damage, power and heat per ship (T030, T033) fit; projectile count is what scales the cost.

## R16: an intercept $10^8$ m from the origin

### Method

`spikes/s6/s6_intercept.gd` (`S6Intercept`) scripts an intercept in the packed world: ship 0 fires one projectile with first-order lead at ship 1, which is 3 km away, thrusting at 2 m/s² and tumbling; ship 2 flies the same inputs as ship 1 and closes on it at 60 m/s to contact. It runs 600 ticks and records the hit tick, the first proximity warning and contact ticks, and the final positions and orientations.

A position is a site id plus a float32 `Vector3` offset (FT-SIM-035). The sim never reads site origins; they live in a float64 table used only to convert to global coordinates (`S6Intercept.world_position`, three GDScript `float`s). The site id still keys the broadphase, so ships at different sites never collide.

`tests/spikes/s6/test_s6_site_local.gd` (gdUnit4, 0.2 s) runs the intercept at site 0 (origin) and at site 1 ($10^8$ m away), and asserts the same hit, warning and contact ticks and bit-identical positions, orientations and projectile position (exact `==`), and that the float64 global positions differ by exactly $10^8$ m. A second test runs the same scenario with naive global float32 positions $10^8$ m out and asserts that it breaks; a third checks that both layouts agree event for event and that a seed repeats.

```
taskset -c 0 tools/gate.sh l1
taskset -c 0 $godot --headless --path . -s res://spikes/s6/precision.gd
```

### Results

| Positions | Hit (tick, ship) | Warning tick | Contact tick | Largest position error after 10 s |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Site-local, origin site | 115, target | 450 | 550 | reference |
| Site-local, site $10^8$ m away | 115, target | 450 | 550 | 0 (bit-identical) |
| Naive float32, $2\times10^4$ m out | 115, target | 450 | 550 | 0.47 m |
| Naive float32, $2\times10^5$ m out | 115, target | 450 | 550 | 1.9 m |
| Naive float32, $10^6$ m out | 115, target | 450 | 550 | 7.5 m |
| Naive float32, $10^7$ m out | 113, target | 450 | 550 | 142 m |
| Naive float32, $10^8$ m out on x | 0, **shooter** | none | none | 1,058 m |
| Naive float32, $10^8$ m out diagonally | none | none | none | 1,073 m |

At $10^8$ m the float32 spacing is 8 m, so a ship moving under 4 m per tick (240 m/s) along that axis never moves, and the projectile's 25 m step rounds to 24 or 32 m. After rounding, the projectile's first step (from a spawn point 27 m ahead of the shooter) passes within the shooter's sphere, so it hits its own ship on tick 0.

### Verdict

**Pass.** With site-local positions the intercept $10^8$ m away is bit-identical to the one at the origin, by construction: the sim only ever sees offsets. The test guards that: any code that adds the site origin inside the sim breaks it.

The same table shows a limit that FT-SIM-035 leaves open. The local offset may reach 200 km, where float32 spacing is 1/64 m, so per-tick displacements are rounded by up to 1/128 m: a ship drifting under about 0.47 m/s along that axis doesn't move at all, and the shooter here (12 m/s) gains 1.9 m in 10 s. At 20 km the threshold is about 0.06 m/s. Combat near a site's centre is fine; slow approach, docking and station-keeping far from it are not. The owner chose 64-bit offsets (D104, below).

## What to promote to T025

- **Data layout:** the packed structure of arrays (`S6WorldPacked`), entities indexed by id, quaternions in a typed `Array[Quaternion]`. Keep per-ship definition fields (mass, inertia, thrust and torque limits) as arrays copied from the definitions (FT-ARC-020).
- **Broadphase:** a uniform grid of 250 m cells keyed by site id and cell coordinates, rebuilt each tick, cells listing ids in order; queries visit only the cells a padded bounding box overlaps, with a shared empty array as the miss default. Projectile sweeps use motion relative to each ship over the tick (T029).
- **Positions:** site id (`int`) plus an offset of three 64-bit floats (D104; the spike used a float32 `Vector3`, which is exact relative to the origin site but too coarse at 200 km); site origins in a float64 table outside the per-tick path, used only for the Relay map and jumps. The grid key packs site and cell coordinates into one `int` (20 bits per axis).
- **Determinism:** one seeded RNG drawn in id order; the layouts' identical checksums show the order is stable (FT-ARC-008, FT-ARC-009).

## Decisions

- Approved in #4 (D104): site-local offsets are three 64-bit floats (GDScript `float`, e.g. a `PackedFloat64Array` per axis); velocities and relative vectors stay float32 `Vector3`. #5 implements it; the cost wasn't measured here, and the 2.6 ms headroom covers it.
- Approved in #4 (D104): the "< 4 ms per tick" budget applies to mean and p99, not every tick; single-tick maxima on a loaded machine reached 4.3 ms.
- The packed layout feeds T025 (provisional): faster than objects by about 20 % at this size and 40 % at twice the size, at the cost of less readable code.
- Collision response, damage and shields are left out of the per-tick work; ships record events only.
